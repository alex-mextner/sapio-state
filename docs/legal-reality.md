# Legal reality and interfaces

Updated: 2026-09-23

Sapio State is currently a research project, not a recognized sovereign state. Software, a membership credential, and community terminology do not by themselves create recognized nationality, jurisdiction, or border-crossing rights.

The 1933 Montevideo Convention is a frequently cited reference in discussions of statehood. It is an Inter-American treaty with a defined set of signatures and ratifications.

Official OAS treaty status:
https://www.oas.org/juridico/english/sigs/a-40.html

Article 2 of the United Nations Charter addresses sovereign equality, peaceful settlement of disputes, and the prohibition on the threat or use of force against the territorial integrity or political independence of states.

Official UN sources:
https://legal.un.org/repertory/art2.shtml
https://www.un.org/en/node/125814

## Global scope and mobility

The project's reference to Earth is a statement of global scope, not a present legal claim over territory governed by existing states.

Article 13 of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights states rights to movement within a state and to leave any country and return to one's own country.

Official UN text:
https://www.un.org/en/node/124305

That text does not create a general entitlement to enter every foreign country. Any future visa-free arrangement for project residents would therefore depend on agreements or rules adopted by the relevant states.

## Passport terminology

Until accepted by competent public authorities, Passport is best treated as a digital membership/residency credential rather than a government travel document.

The credential can still support verifiable claims and network entitlements without making claims about current border acceptance.

## Rules as software

The project can represent policy using human-readable text, machine-readable rules, tests, version history, simulations, and implementation code.

Existing legal systems determine what has legal force. Executable policy is therefore an implementation and audit mechanism unless a competent jurisdiction formally adopts it.

## Open digital infrastructure

The UN Global Digital Compact recognizes open-source software, open data, open AI models, open standards, and open content as forms of digital public goods when applicable safeguards are met.

Official UN text:
https://www.un.org/pact-for-the-future/en/annex-i-global-digital-compact

This provides public-policy context for open digital infrastructure; it is not recognition of this project.

## Work requiring jurisdiction-specific review

Before production deployment, the project should obtain qualified advice on entity structure, identity credentials, privacy/data-protection roles, consumer terms, dispute resolution, any token/payment design, and country-specific travel or residency representations.
