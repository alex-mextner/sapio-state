# Recognition, agreements, and mobility roadmap

Status: long-term research roadmap

This document does not assume that Sapio State currently has sovereign status or that states are obligated to recognize its credentials.

## Phase 0 — digital community

Deliverables:
- open governance specifications;
- portable residency credential;
- privacy/security architecture;
- dispute process;
- transparent legal entity for contracts;
- clear public statements about current legal status.

Goal: prove the digital institution can operate without making claims about territory or border access.

## Phase 1 — service interoperability

Seek ordinary agreements with:
- universities;
- research institutions;
- employers;
- merchants;
- conferences;
- local service providers.

Passport can begin as a membership/eligibility credential that participating organizations voluntarily verify.

## Phase 2 — public-sector pilots

Possible pilots with willing public authorities:
- credential interoperability;
- researcher/entrepreneur programmes;
- digital public services;
- open-source e-government components;
- trusted-traveller experiments within existing law.

Each pilot remains under the participating authority's legal framework.

## Phase 3 — mobility agreements

A visa-free or simplified-entry regime can only exist where relevant states create it.

Potential negotiated forms:
- visa waivers;
- e-visa fast lanes;
- research/talent mobility programmes;
- residence/work permits;
- trusted-traveller agreements;
- mutual recognition of selected credentials.

The Universal Declaration of Human Rights Article 13 protects movement within a state and the right to leave a country and return to one's own country; it is not a universal foreign-entry guarantee.

Official UN text:
https://www.un.org/en/node/124305

## Phase 4 — formal international relationships

If the project ever seeks a public-international-law status, that becomes a separate legal and diplomatic programme.

Relevant context includes:
- UN Charter principles, including sovereign equality and peaceful settlement:
  https://legal.un.org/repertory/art2.shtml
- the Montevideo Convention as a classic Inter-American reference:
  https://www.oas.org/juridico/english/sigs/a-40.html

No milestone in this repository should be described as recognition unless the recognizing authority has actually taken that action.

## "Ratification by all countries"

This is treated as a long-term vision for negotiated acceptance, not as a forecast.

A useful measurable ladder is:
1. organizations accepting Passport as membership proof;
2. municipalities/public agencies piloting credentials;
3. national authorities recognizing specific credential claims;
4. mobility agreements;
5. broader treaty relationships.

Progress should be reported as signed/active agreements rather than rhetorical support.

## Peaceful-only constraint

The project's international strategy is limited to consent, contracts, public law, agreements, and diplomacy.

The UN Charter requires member states to settle international disputes peacefully and refrain from the threat or use of force against other states' territorial integrity or political independence.

Official UN Charter:
https://www.un.org/en/node/125814
